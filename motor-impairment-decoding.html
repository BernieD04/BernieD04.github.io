EEG-Based Motor Impairment Decoding

Jan - May 2026 NE 412, Neural Signal Analysis Eight-author project

Stroke recovery is scored by hand, by a trained clinician, on a subjective scale. This project tested how far EEG from sensorimotor cortex gets toward doing that objectively.

The Fugl-Meyer Assessment is the standard measure of upper-limb motor function after a stroke. It is also subjective, takes a trained clinician to administer, and cannot be run continuously or outside a clinic. An EEG-derived measure tracking the same thing would remove all three constraints, which is the motivation for looking for one.

Data and tasks

EEG from 21 subjects, 14 stroke survivors and 7 healthy controls, recorded in Yuan Yang's lab at Illinois. Subjects held shoulder abduction at two effort levels, 20% and 40% of maximum voluntary contraction. That gave two classification questions: stroke against control, and low effort against high effort.

Analysis was restricted to sensorimotor electrodes (FC1 to FC6, C1 to C6, CP1 to CP6, and the midline FCz, Cz, CPz). Those sit over primary motor and somatosensory cortex, where the mu and beta rhythms that respond to movement are generated. Dropping the rest cuts noise from task-irrelevant regions and keeps the feature count down, which matters particularly at 21 subjects.

Features

Four types, all computed in MNE-Python over one-second epochs:

  • Power spectral density, by Welch's method, over low beta (13 to 20 Hz) and high beta (20 to 30 Hz). Local activity.
  • Coherence between channel pairs. Network-level synchronization.
  • Imaginary coherence, which keeps only the time-lagged part and so discards the zero-lag coupling that volume conduction produces rather than real communication between regions.
  • Phase lag, the timing offset between channel pairs.

These were combined into five feature sets, from PSD alone up to everything at once, and each set was run through both a logistic regression and a random forest. The aim was not to identify a single best model but to determine which feature types carry the signal.

Guarding against leakage

With 21 subjects and many epochs each, splitting epochs at random would place the same subject in both the training and test sets, producing an accuracy that largely reflects subject identity. The split was made at subject level, 80/20, so every epoch from a given person sits on one side only. Accuracy came from 5-fold stratified grouped cross-validation, which keeps both the class balance and the subject grouping intact, and feature standardization was fitted inside each fold rather than across the whole set.

Best on stroke against control was the random forest on PSD plus phase lag, at 0.695 accuracy and an AUC of 0.59. Effort level performed near chance: the best result was 0.575 with an AUC of 0.56, and several feature sets fell at or below chance. Two low-effort contractions do not separate at the group level with these features.

What the feature sets showed

The more informative result is the shape of the table rather than the best single number. Adding coherence and then imaginary coherence made things consistently worse, with the random forest falling from 0.665 on PSD alone to 0.615 and then 0.575. Adding phase lag instead pushed it up to 0.695.

Adding features did not improve performance. Each connectivity measure adds a pair for every channel combination, and against 21 subjects that added dimensionality produces overfitting faster than signal. Phase lag held up where coherence did not, plausibly because discounting zero-lag coupling removes much of what volume conduction contributes, leaving fewer and less redundant features.

The two models also relied on different features. Feature importance put PSD far ahead for the random forest, while the logistic regression spread its weight across the connectivity measures, which fits the random forest doing better on the grosser stroke-versus-control split and the logistic regression edging ahead on the subtler effort task.

Honest read

An AUC of 0.59 is above chance, but not by much, and the error bars across folds are wide enough to reflect real inter-subject variability. With 14 stroke survivors and 7 controls, a group-level model is being asked to generalize across people whose post-stroke reorganization differs individually. The result supports EEG carrying usable information about motor impairment; it does not support a clinical tool.
